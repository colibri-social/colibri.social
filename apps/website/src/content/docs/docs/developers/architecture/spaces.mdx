Colibri is built on **ATproto spaces**. Every other page in this section assumes you know what they are, so start here. The developer docs also assume you are familiar with the AT Protocol as a concept and related terminology. For more information, see [atproto.com](https://atproto.com).

An ATproto space is a shared, access-controlled view over records that live
in their authors' own repositories. A group of repositories agrees on who may
read them, and that agreement is the space. **A space is logical, not physical**.

## How access works

The data of a Colibri Space is gated at the protocol level, by the PDS that
hosts the Space's account. A reader without a credential for an ATproto
space receives no records from it.

There is **no relay for permissioned data**, so an AppView has no firehose of
Space content to subscribe to. It pulls each member's repository from that
member's own host, presenting a credential for the space.
See [Sync](/docs/developers/architecture/sync).

A member whose PDS does not serve the `com.atproto.space` methods keeps their
space repositories on the AppView until the PDS reports the methods exist. See
[Hosting](/docs/developers/architecture/hosting).

## The three parties

| Party | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Authority** | Owns the space and decides who may read it. For a Colibri Space, this is the Space's own DID. |
| **Repo host** | Holds a member's repository and serves their records to whoever presents a valid credential. |
| **Reader** | Anything that wants to read the space. The AppView is one, and a client can be another. |

A space is identified by its authority, a space type, and a key:

```
at://{authority}/space/{spaceType}/{skey}
```

The space type is an NSID naming a lexicon whose `main` definition is a space
declaration. It lists the record collections the space may hold and carries an
optional human-readable `title`, which is what an OAuth consent screen shows a
user. Without a `title`, the consent screen shows the NSID.

A record inside a space needs the author as well, because record keys are unique
per repository and a space aggregates many repositories:

```
at://{authority}/space/{spaceType}/{skey}/{author}/{collection}/{rkey}
```

Colibri message and reaction references carry an author DID alongside the record
key. A bare key is ambiguous inside a space.

## Getting a credential

Reading a space requires a **space credential** issued by the authority.
Obtaining one is a three-party exchange.

<Steps>

1. **A delegation token is minted by the reader's own PDS** with
   `com.atproto.space.getDelegationToken`. It is single use and short-lived.

2. **The token is presented to the authority** with
   `com.atproto.space.authorizeSpaceSync`, along with a DPoP proof and the
   key the credential binds to. The authority decides here whether to issue a
   credential, and answers `{ credential, spaceRev? }`.

3. **The credential is presented to each repo host** in the space, with a fresh
   DPoP proof naming that host.

</Steps>

Calling `authorizeSpaceSync` again before the credential expires reauthorizes.
A reader that keeps a copy of the space, such as the AppView, also registers for
notifications in the same call. See
[Sync](/docs/developers/architecture/sync).

### Verifying responses

A repo host proves that a commit or record came from the author's repository
with a MAC. The reader asks for one with an `Accept-Signature` header on
`getLatestCommit`, `getRepo`, `listRepoOps` and `getRecord`. The host answers
with an HTTP Message Signature in the `Signature-Input` and `Signature` headers,
covering a header that carries the commit or the record.

The MAC key is a secret derived from the author's `#atproto_kem` key and a
fresh key share the reader sends with the request. The reader derives a new
secret for every request.

### App passwords

`com.atproto.space.getDelegationToken` requires full account access, which app
passwords do not carry. Minting a delegation token needs either an OAuth
session or a session opened with the account's real password.

The AppView generates and stores an encrypted password for each Space account
it creates, and it stores an encrypted OAuth session for a Space account that
its owner connected by signing in. A client mints a delegation token through its
OAuth session before it grants the AppView access to the user's personal space
with `social.colibri.beta.actor.grantSpaceAccess`.

## Policies

An authority answers credential requests according to the space's **policy**.
Colibri uses two policy types.

**`com.atproto.memberspace.policy`** admits a list of members, with optional
flags such as `publicRead`. A user's personal space uses it with no members. The
space host authorizes the owner before it consults any policy, so the owner is
the only reader.

**`com.atproto.managedspace.policy`** delegates the decision to an application.
Every ATproto space in a Colibri Space uses this, the profile space included,
naming the AppView as `managingApp`. When someone asks the Space's PDS for a credential, the PDS
calls
[`com.atproto.managedspace.checkAccess`](/docs/developers/specification/appview) on the
AppView and follows the answer. The AppView answers `{ read, write }`.

Only members can read the profile space. The AppView reads it with the Space's
own credentials and keeps the Space's name, description and images in its
index. It serves them from there to people who haven't joined, for example in an
invitation preview, along with signed links to the images.

A ban is the AppView answering `read: false` when the Space's PDS asks whether
that person may read. The refusal happens at the protocol layer, before any
data moves.

:::caution[The AppView must be reachable]
A managed-space policy makes the AppView's reachability a hard requirement.
When a Space's PDS cannot reach the AppView, it answers credential requests for
that Space's ATproto spaces with `UpstreamFailure`, and the Space is
unreadable while its PDS is up. **This may change in the future**.

The write path has a consequence of its own. See
[the writer set](/docs/developers/architecture/sync#the-writer-set).
:::

## One AppView per Space

A Colibri Space is bound to the AppView that manages it. The Space's record
names that AppView in its `managingApp` field. Only the managing AppView holds
the Space's credentials and answers `checkAccess` for it, so every read, write,
event and voice call for that Space goes through that AppView.

See [Where a Space Is Hosted](/docs/users/spaces/appviews) for what
that means when you belong to Spaces on more than one.
